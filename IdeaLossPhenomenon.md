# The Idea Loss Phenomenon: Statistical Evidence of Creativity's Paradox in the AI Era

## Abstract

Right before our eyes, a "loss of knowledge" on a scale unprecedented in human history is quietly unfolding.

Hundreds of millions of people converse and exchange thoughts with AI every week. Brainstorming concepts, refining texts, exploring solutions, verbalizing business ideas—this "externalization of thought" has become an everyday infrastructure on an unparalleled scale.

However, for almost all the inspirations born there, nothing happens.

Nothing is done at the moment of conception. Nothing is done even if saved. They merely drift through chat logs, eventually fading from everyone's memory. A massive volume of "uncut gems of thought," far surpassing the burning of the Library of Alexandria or any historical book burning, is born daily and evaporates without even a wisp of smoke. Yet, no one counts the lost ideas. Therefore, this unprecedented loss has not even had a name until now.

This paper names this quietly progressing structural loss the **"Idea Loss Phenomenon,"** and clarifies its scale and structure based on public primary sources from 2025–2026.

## Introduction

The rapid development of generative AI has fundamentally altered the structure of human creativity. However, this transformation has created a profound paradox.

The hurdle of verbalizing an inspiration into a concrete form has been dramatically lowered. Fragments of ideas that once remained trapped in the mind now routinely gain form and words through dialogue with AI. Yet, beyond the ideas that have thus gained form, there is almost nothing.

For individuals, the cost and risk of executing their own ideas remain high. Most importantly, in many cases, they do not even think to "execute." The moment they think "this is interesting," the conversation shifts to the next topic, and the inspiration sinks into the flow of the chat. A few days later, they forget it even existed. For someone else—an entrepreneur with funding and execution capability, a corporation, or a researcher—it might have been a "gem" that could generate immense value, but it disappears without ever catching anyone's eye.

This paper defines the "Idea Loss Phenomenon" in two layers. The first layer is **"Uncaptured Loss"**—the phenomenon where inspirations born in the flow of dialogue with generative AI disappear into chat logs before being consciously captured and saved. The second layer is **"Unexecuted Loss"**—the phenomenon where even consciously recognized and recorded ideas are abandoned, frozen, or left to rot without being realized due to constraints such as execution capability, funding, personnel, and time. Encompassing both, this paper defines the **"Idea Loss Phenomenon."** The spread of generative AI did not newly create this phenomenon, but rather acted as a catalyst to expand it explosively.

## Externalization of Thought Has Become a Daily Infrastructure

To understand the scale of the Idea Loss Phenomenon, we must first grasp "how many people are exchanging how many thoughts with AI."

Generative AI, which reached a 53% population penetration rate in just three years (Stanford HAI, 2026), has now settled into a daily infrastructure where hundreds of millions of people exchange thoughts weekly. Neither the personal computer, the internet, nor the smartphone—none of their adoption curves match this velocity.

The scale of ChatGPT's usage vividly illustrates this rapid entrenchment. ChatGPT, which had approximately 250 million weekly active users in the fall of 2024, reached 700 million in July 2025, and came to process **2.5 billion dialogues per day**—about 29,000 messages per second (OpenAI & Harvard, 2025). By February 2026, it reached 900 million (TechCrunch, 2026), and in August 2026, OpenAI officially announced it had surpassed 1 billion weekly active users (Yahoo Tech, 2026). In human history, no other consumer service has reached the 1-billion-user mark at this speed.

It is not limited to ChatGPT. Gemini (Google) recorded 35 million daily active users on its app alone (as of April 2025, TechCrunch, 2025) and continues to expand rapidly. Claude (Anthropic) is even more dramatic, with its mobile app daily active users surging 183% from about 4 million in early 2026 to 11.3 million just two months later in March (Similarweb via TechCrunch, 2026). Daily dialogue with AI is no longer a phenomenon of a single platform; it is taking root across multiple services at an accelerating pace.

In OECD countries, over a third of individuals used generative AI as of 2025, with usage rates reaching 41.1% among workers and approximately 75% among students (OECD, 2026). Private AI investment in 2025 recorded a 127.5% year-over-year increase, and over a 200% increase when limited to generative AI, with private AI investment reaching $285.9 billion in the US alone (Stanford HAI, 2026). In terms of both people and money, dialogue with generative AI is already part of society's foundational infrastructure.

## What is Happening Within Those Dialogues?

What is crucial is what people are doing with AI.

According to a joint study by OpenAI and Harvard University (2025), the primary usage categories of ChatGPT are "Practical Guidance," "Writing," and "Seeking Information," and these three categories alone account for **about 78% of all messages**. This is not mere search behavior. Formulating concepts, organizing problems, summarizing texts—that is, the **"externalization of thought"** is being conducted in 2.5 billion dialogues every day.

The fact that about 70% of consumer usage is for non-work purposes is noteworthy. The dialogue of thought with AI has transcended occupational contexts and permeated individuals' daily lives. People are talking to AI not just for work, but to deepen, organize, and verbalize their own thoughts.

**The improvement in creative productivity is also striking.** A joint study by Harvard Business School and Boston Consulting Group demonstrated that the work quality of consultants using AI improved by up to 40%, and the task completion speed increased by about 25% (Dell'Acqua et al., 2023). In the software development field, developers using GitHub Copilot reportedly completed tasks about 56% faster in controlled experiments (Peng et al., 2023), and there are reports that the proportion of code written by AI coding assistance tools reaches around 40% depending on the project. In April 2025, using OpenAI's image generation feature, not 1.3 million but **over 130 million** users generated more than 700 million images in just the first week (OpenAI, 2025).

What these numbers indicate is that every day, within billions of dialogues, an enormous volume of inspirations is born, verbalized, and substantiated. So what happens to those inspirations afterward?

## No One Counts the Lost Ideas

In almost all cases, nothing happens.

An idea that felt "potentially interesting" in a dialogue with AI. How many people pick it up, save it, show it to someone, and put it into practice? Almost no one. This is not laziness, but a rational judgment. Against the cost of implementing a single idea into society—time, funding, risk, expertise—the return an individual can obtain is simply not worth it. Therefore, the moment they think "this is interesting," the conversation moves to the next topic. A few days later, they forget that inspiration even existed.

**Herein lies the peculiar nature of the Idea Loss Phenomenon. No one can count the lost ideas.** They may technically remain in chat logs, but no one reads back through them. They are never systematically evaluated. The means to know "what that inspiration at that time could have brought to the world" are permanently lost.

This is exactly why there are no direct statistics. What has disappeared cannot be measured. However, its existence is strongly suggested indirectly. Hundreds of millions of people exchange thoughts with AI every week, 2.5 billion dialogues occur daily, and 78% of them are spent on the externalization of thought. Of the inspirations generated daily at this scale, how many are consciously captured, saved, and lead to some kind of action? It can be rationally estimated that the answer to that question is "a tiny fraction."

In human history, the "loss of knowledge" has always been remembered as a visible tragedy caused by violence, such as the burning of the Library of Alexandria or the burning of books. But the modern loss of knowledge takes a completely different, quieter, and more massive form. There is no smoke, no suppression. Countless inspirations born casually in everyday dialogues simply sink to the bottom of chat logs and evaporate. There may be those who mourn burned books, but no one mourns the ideas that vanish unnoticed at the bottom of a chat. Yet, the total volume of thought being lost far surpasses the entire collection of every library in the past.

The reason this paper gives the name "Idea Loss" to this phenomenon is that a problem without a name cannot become a subject of discussion.

## For Organizations, the Loss of Ideas is No Exception

The Idea Loss Phenomenon is not limited to the daily lives of individuals. Even corporations, which supposedly possess resources and organizational capability, are experiencing the same structural loss.

**A 2025 survey by S&P Global / 451 Research** (targeting 1,006 IT and business professionals) revealed that an average of **46% of AI projects are abandoned** between PoC and production deployment. The percentage of companies abandoning the majority of their AI initiatives surged to more than double in one year, from 17% the previous year to 42%. The primary reasons for abandonment cited by companies are cost overruns, data privacy, and security risks, with over a quarter (27%) citing a lack of skills as a challenge (S&P Global, 2025)—all pointing not to defects in the ideas or the technology itself, but to the limits of resources to execute them.

**Deloitte's 2026 survey** (targeting 3,235 AI executives) also reports that **only 25% of companies were able to transition more than 40% of their AI experiments to production environments.** **McKinsey (2025)** indicates that while 88% of organizations use AI for business, **about two-thirds of companies have not yet begun scaling it across the enterprise.**

**The World Economic Forum (WEF) 2026 report** points out that despite **over $250 billion being invested** in AI globally in 2025, **only 25% of companies achieved transformative impact.** In a **2026 survey by the UK Government (DSIT)**, while 75% of AI-adopting companies answered that "productivity improved," **77% of companies saw no change in revenue.**

**In Japan, this structure appears particularly sharply.** According to the IPA (Information-technology Promotion Agency) "DX Trends 2026" (July 2026, targeting 1,799 companies), nearly 80% of Japanese companies are engaging in DX in some form, and 60% report "producing results." However, looking at the specific uses of AI, while applications aimed at business efficiency such as "summarization, translation, and proofreading of documents/audio" (82.5%) and "creation of documents/reports" (80.5%) are prominent, "planning support for production/logistics/service provision" is a mere 6.0%, and "advancement of own products/services" remains at 10.9%. Regarding the introduction effects, while 91.6% answered that "business became more efficient and faster," only 3.9% answered that "sales or profits improved," and 4.5% that "customer satisfaction improved"——**there is a gap of more than ten times between efficiency improvement and value creation** (IPA, 2026). AI is making work faster, but it is not creating new value.

Even corporations—entities equipped with budgets, personnel, and organizational capability—fail to fully execute the vast majority of their ideas and experiments, leaving them to rot on the shelf. It is rather natural that individuals, with only their own resources, cannot put the inspirations born from dialogue with AI into practice.

## The Essence of the Idea Loss Phenomenon

Looking at the above, the essence of the Idea Loss Phenomenon comes into view.

It is neither a lack of technology nor a problem with the quality of the ideas. It means that **an unprecedented disconnect has emerged between the ability to generate inspirations and the ability to give them form.**

AI has brought the hurdle of the "externalization of thought" infinitely close to zero. However, the mechanisms beyond the externalization of thought—picking it up, evaluating it, executing it, and turning it into value—have not changed at all. As the data from Japanese companies plainly shows, even as AI adoption progresses, the path to transcend the wall of "efficiency improvement" and reach "value creation" is hardly established. As a result, almost all of the inspirations born from billions of daily dialogues disappear without passing into anyone's hands.

Humanity has a job market that liquefies labor, a financial market that liquefies capital, and a real estate market that liquefies real estate. However, there is nowhere for "unformed ideas and thoughts" to flow. For both individuals and corporations, the only destination for ideas that cannot be realized is "oblivion."

## Conclusion

The "Idea Loss Phenomenon" presented in this paper is a problem that no one has named until now because it cannot be directly observed. Lost ideas do not appear in statistics. However, now that there are hundreds of millions of AI users weekly and 2.5 billion dialogues daily, the scale of the loss silently progressing behind it is incomparable to any loss of knowledge in the past.

The corporate data presented in this paper serves as material to reinforce this inference, not replace it. If even corporations with budgets, personnel, and organizational capabilities cannot overcome the wall of execution, it is all the more natural that individuals cannot single-handedly give form to inspirations born from dialogue with AI.

This paper does not present a solution to the problem. It proposes a concept to recognize the existence of the problem.

However, I would like to touch upon one possibility. **If one could "release" an idea they cannot realize themselves, and send it to a place where it catches the eye of someone who can**—in that process, the idea might be evaluated from others' perspectives, polished, and eventually reach the hands of an appropriate executor. Just as the stock market brought a "price discovery function" to capital, an idea circulation infrastructure could bring a "feasibility discovery function" to inspiration.

The transition from an era where ideas are lost to an era where ideas are circulated—exploring that possibility might be the new question born in the AI era.
